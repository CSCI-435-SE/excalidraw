# Sprint Overview

The team planned to complete 17 issues during Sprint 1. Ten of these were subissues of two larger issues (#18 and #12), while another three were intended to begin work on a third larger issue (#32).

The team delivered several features across grid customization, motion paths and animation, gradient styling and export, and text hyperlinks. Most work again this week focused on front-end Additional work included AI usage documentation and repository-wide linting and formatting fixes.

Although substantial functionality was merged, some planned issues remain unfinished. The team also encountered challenges with some trivial checks that were solved and the timing of work tied to busy schedules. Continual learning of the complexities of agentic AI use in the workspace of PRs allows us to become more streamlined and continue to improve on our guidelines to properly analyze code for understanding gaps generated by an agentic/human hybrid coding style.

#Test Strategy

Our team used automated tests alongside individual implementation and review of tests to check new behavior and its functionality against pre-existing code. Test cases made by ai were reviewed line by line to understand the implementation, alongside explanation by the agent to understand coverage.
Testing included:
Unit and component tests for new functionality.
Regression testing for existing editor behavior.
Export tests for gradient and SVG behavior.
Validation of configuration, persistence, and compatibility where applicable.
CI checks for typechecking, linting, formatting, and automated tests.
The linting issue has been fixed, with bucket size being the only CI check that still fails. Repo-side issue, a workaround will be looked into before next sprint, although the assumptions made in the check may be impossible to work around.

# Sprint 2 Plan

During Sprint 2, our primary goal is to have all team members focus on completing multiple remaining major issues, minimum of 2 and the rest of Issue #32 and their associated sub-issues, particularly larger features that require additional implementation and integration. We will also prioritize reviewing and improving the existing backend code to ensure the system is stable, maintainable, and ready for future development.

By completing these foundational tasks, we aim to prepare the project for the next major phase: implementing user authority and permission management within client-to-client encrypted servers. This will involve establishing the backend functionality needed to manage user roles, control access to server resources, and enforce permissions while preserving the security and privacy of encrypted communication.

Throughout the sprint, team members will coordinate on issue ownership, divide larger tasks into manageable sub-issues, and regularly review progress to identify blockers and ensure compatibility between features. By the end of Sprint 2, we aim to have completed as many outstanding major issues as possible related to incomplete front-end tasks, strengthen the backend architecture, and established a solid foundation for developing user authority and permission controls in the subsequent phase. Scoping back-end and its complexities will be a main focus to properly benchmark the size of client-to-client issues.

Due to problems in this sprint issue 40 will be carried over to sprint 2
